# Optional ticket email delivery

The buyer explicitly selects ticket email during checkout. A signed-in buyer can use their verified linked email or prove another destination with a `checkout_email` code tied to the initiating session. A guest can verify an email in the anonymous session and buy without Telegram or an account. Email verification for delivery never links an account. The order stores the normalized recipient and request timestamp, and a replay with the same idempotency key but a changed destination conflicts. Profile changes do not redirect an existing order.

Only committed paid settlement appends `ticket.email_requested`; free purchases use the same settlement method. A partial unique outbox index and one `TicketEmailDelivery` row per order prevent duplicate initial jobs and historical paid orders receive no backfill. The API worker claims short leases, checks payment/ticket eligibility again before sending, and submits a stable provider idempotency key outside the payment transaction. Rate responses retry with bounded backoff; ambiguous timeouts and expired leases become `uncertain` after the safe retry window and require an authorized manual resend. Payment, ticket issuance, and inventory settlement do not depend on Resend availability. A manual resend keeps the original recipient, rotates the access capability, has a ten-minute cooldown, and is capped at five generations. `accepted` means Resend accepted the request, not that the recipient received or read it. Provider-side bounces remain visible in Resend until a webhook/reconciliation workflow is added.

The Russian message states the frozen event date, time, timezone, venue and item labels, and distinguishes a paid deposit from full payment. It links to `/delivery#token=...`, never to a JWT, quick-session/access token, claim token, or QR secret. The browser removes the fragment before requesting data by POST; the page and API responses disable caching/referrers. The server stores only a SHA-256 hash of the HMAC-derived, order/generation-bound bearer token. Access expires after 30 days, is revoked on resend, and permits only that paid purchase's ticket/group-pass view and QR images. The view reads current ticket states; cancelled or refunded tickets cannot yield QR assets. GET link scanners do not consume access. The bearer capability is still sensitive: recipients should avoid forwarding the message.

Anonymous order claim remains separate: a purchase/claim capability and an authenticated account with the matching verified email (or original Telegram identity for Telegram purchases) are both required. Email recipients of registered buyers cannot claim the buyer's order. The existing quick access capability and Telegram bot delivery remain independent.

Configure the verified `mail.ticketron.live` sending domain, `RESEND_API_KEY`, `RESEND_FROM_EMAIL`, a separate 32+ character `OTP_HMAC_SECRET`, and the final HTTPS `WEB_ORIGIN`; the access link follows `WEB_ORIGIN`, so changing from ngrok to `ticketron.live` requires updating this value before new sends. Keep old origin available until previously emailed links expire or resend them after cutover. Limit provider credentials to sending access. Keep a single API worker or rely on database leases across instances, monitor queued/failed/uncertain jobs and Resend bounces, and set sending quotas with the provider. Without Resend, email verification and email jobs explicitly remain unavailable; no fake success is reported. SMS regions/provider remain unselected.

The migration is additive and has no historical-email backfill. A rollback to Telegram-only application code is unsafe after non-Telegram accounts or email-only purchases exist; roll forward or restore a deliberate backup. Verification challenges/grants are cleaned after their bounded retention window. Ticket capabilities expire after 30 days; delivery rows remain as purchase/audit history. Tests use fake senders and isolated recipients only; live Resend acceptance and inbox delivery have not been asserted.
